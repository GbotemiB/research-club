Manifesto

Anyone can do research. Together, we can do more.

Research never ends. There are always more gaps to find. I’m here to fill this one, so together we can find more gaps and fill them.

Vision

Every CMU-Africa student who wants to do research can find a way in, and people to do it with.

Mission

Make research easier to start and easier to finish, by connecting students, faculty, courses and partners.

Building on what we have

The Research Club has built a lot: the SRC Hub (Student Research Collaboration Hub), the Research Showcase, the conference list, workshop recordings, the newsletter, and events with almost every club on campus. I want to build on that, not start over.

What I see, and what I will do

What I see

Good tools exist, but many students don't know about them.

What I will do

Make it easy to find

  • A simple "Start here" guide for new students that points to the SRC Hub, the Showcase and the workshop recordings.
  • Share the club's tools at the start of every semester, not only at events.
  • Keep the Projects page and a monthly spotlight up to date, building on the newsletter.
What I see

It is hard to know who is working on what.

What I will do

Connect people

  • A Faculty Research Directory: what each professor works on and what help they need, updated every month by student volunteers.
  • A student interest map and focus groups, to find people with the same interests.
  • Voluntary mentoring: second-year students help first-year students start research.
What I see

Research waits for summer, and good work ends as drafts.

What I will do

From class project to paper

  • Use the Research Showcase to find strong course projects, and the SRC Hub to keep them going after the course ends.
  • Continue the focus groups and writing clinics, timed before big deadlines.
  • Keep the conference list up to date automatically, with African venues and workshops added. See the preview.

Swipe to see all three.

How you will know it is working

At the end of each semester, I will share a short update on:

  • How many faculty are in the directory, and whether it is kept up to date
  • How many students joined the interest map, focus groups and the SRC Hub
  • How many course projects continued after the course ended
  • What students say is still not working

About me

Speech, energy, and people who build together

I am a first-year master's student in Engineering Artificial Intelligence at Carnegie Mellon University Africa (CMU-Africa) in Kigali. Before that, I studied Computer Engineering at Obafemi Awolowo University in Nigeria.

My research covers two areas. The first is speech and language technology for low-resource African languages, especially Yoruba and Yoruba-English code-switched speech (speech that moves between the two languages). The second is open-source energy system modelling for national energy plans.

Most of my work has been done in teams, with friends, colleagues and classmates. That is why my campaign is built around collaboration.

Yoruba-English speech recognition

Co-led the collection of 100 hours of Yoruba-English code-switched speech. Fine-tuned Whisper and MMS (Massively Multilingual Speech) models, cutting word error rate from 72.6% to 32.2%.
