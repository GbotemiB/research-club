About me

Speech, energy, and people who build together

I study for an MS in Engineering Artificial Intelligence at Carnegie Mellon University Africa in Kigali. Before CMU-Africa, I studied Computer Engineering at Obafemi Awolowo University in Nigeria.

My research covers two areas. The first is speech and language technology for low-resource African languages, especially Yoruba and Yoruba-English code-switched speech (speech that moves between the two languages). The second is open-source energy system modelling for national energy plans.

Most of my work has been done in teams, with friends, colleagues and classmates. That is why my campaign is built around collaboration.

Manifesto

Anyone can do research. Together, we can do more.

Vision

Every CMU-Africa student who wants to do research can find a way in, and people to do it with.

Mission

Make research easier to start and easier to finish, by connecting students, faculty, courses and partners.

Building on what we have

The Research Club has done a lot. It has run events with almost every club on campus, published the newsletter, and built real interest from faculty and alumni. I want to build on that, not start over.

What I see

It is hard to know who is working on what

Students don't always know which faculty need help, or which classmates share their interests.

Research often waits for summer

Balancing coursework and research at the same time is not easy for everyone.

Good work ends as drafts

Many course projects and proposals stop when the semester ends.

What I will do

  1. Know who is doing what

    • A Faculty Research Directory on the club website: what each professor is working on, what help they need, and their current lab. Student volunteers will update it every month.
    • A student research interest map, so people can find others with the same interests and join focus groups. Nobody is forced; this is for people who want to take part.

  2. Use the courses we already have

    • A list of courses at CMU-Africa that teach research skills, with Research Methods at the top.
    • Work with these courses so that good proposals don't end as proposals, and students get help to continue them.

  3. From project to paper continue and grow

    • Continue and grow the focus groups and writing clinics the club already runs.
    • Help students move good projects from draft to submission.

  4. A conference tracker

    • A tracker on the club website that shows upcoming conferences and their deadlines, including those on OpenReview, so nobody misses a deadline. See the preview.

  5. Mixed teams

    • Second-year students who are willing can mentor first-year students on research. Mentoring is voluntary.

  6. Show our work continue and grow

    • An archive of research done at CMU-Africa.
    • A monthly spotlight on the website and LinkedIn, building on the newsletter.

  7. Work toward links with CMU Pittsburgh and CMU Portugal

    • I will start by finding contact people on both campuses and learning how students there can work with us.

How you will know it is working

At the end of each semester, I will share a short update on:

  • How many faculty are in the directory, and whether it is kept up to date
  • How many students joined the interest map and focus groups
  • How many projects continued after a course ended
  • What students say is still not working
